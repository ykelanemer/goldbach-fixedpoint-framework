## Executive Summary

This repository contains the formal mathematical manuscript establishing an arithmetic-dynamical and spectral fixed-point framework ($P^*_\infty$) that reformulates hypothetical binary Goldbach counterexamples $2N > 6$ into an isolated, finite **Linear-Algebraic & Diophantine Matrix System** $\mathcal{S}(2N, k, M)$.

Instead of analyzing prime pairs additively through classical circle or sieve methods, the framework analyzes the divisor orbit generated by prime complements $2N - p$. Under the counterexample hypothesis, the iterated prime divisor mapping $D(S) = \bigcup_{p \in S} \{ q \in \mathbb{P} \mid q \mid (2N - p) \}$ converges in finitely many steps to a non-empty stationary limit set $P^*_\infty = D(P^*_\infty)$ bounded strictly inside $\mathcal{P}_{\le \frac{2N-5}{3}}$.

### Core Contributions

1. **Complete Unconditional Low-Dimensional Elimination ($k \le 5$):** Unconditional non-existence of fixed-point islands for all dimensions $k \in \{0, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5\}$, as well as for all sparse square-free exponent domains across all dimensions ($k \ge 4$).
2. **Discovery of Multiplicative Structural Invariants:**
   - **Coprime Base Complement Theorem:** The two smallest complements share zero prime factors from the island ($\gcd(2N - p_1, 2N - p_2) = 1$ in $I$), forcing disjoint prime supports $S_1 \cap S_2 = \emptyset$.
   - **Universal Maximal Prime In-Degree Rigidity:** The maximal prime $p_k = \max(I)$ divides at most one complement in $I$ with exponent $a_{m,k} = 1$, inducing an exact eigenvector decoupling ratio $u_k = \frac{u_m}{\rho(M)} \le \frac{u_m}{2}$.
   - **Perron Geometric Mean Ceiling:** The left Perron eigenvector $\mathbf{u} > \mathbf{0}$ enforces $\prod_{j=1}^k p_j^{u_j} < (2N)^{1/\rho(M)} \le \sqrt{2N}$.
3. **Inductive Node Contraction & Asymptotic Dimensional Ceiling ($k \ge 98$ Elimination):** By developing an inductive Schur-type node contraction preserving total exponent mass ($\sum_{i,j} A_{i,j} \ge 2k$), high-dimensional islands compress down to dimension 5, forcing the mean row sum to satisfy $\overline{S}_5 \ge \frac{2k}{5}$. This yields an absolute dimensional bound $k \le \frac{5}{2} \lfloor \frac{\ln(2N)}{\ln 3} \rfloor \approx 97$, unconditionally ruling out all high-dimensional fixed-point islands ($k \ge 98$ for $2N \approx 4 \cdot 10^{18}$).
4. **Structural Reduction to a Finite Frontier ($6 \le k \le 97$):** Hypothetical counterexamples are strictly confined to a narrow, finite integer window of dimensions $6 \le k \le 97$ with $p_2 \le \sqrt{2N}$, where the two coprime base factorizations satisfy an ultra-tight logarithmic coincidence $|\Lambda_{1,2}| < 10^{-9}$.

## Story of the Framework

### 1. The Counterexample Setup
Suppose Goldbach's Conjecture is false for some even integer $2N > 6$. Then for **every** odd prime $p < 2N$ (with $p \nmid 2N$), the complement $2N - p$ can **never** be prime; every complement must be a composite number factored into at least two primes.

### 2. The Iterated Prime Divisor Operator ($D$)
Starting from $P^*(0) = \{ p \in \mathbb{P} \mid 3 \le p \le 2N-3, p \nmid 2N\}$, we define the prime divisor operator:
\[
D(S) = \bigcup_{p \in S} \{ q \in \mathbb{P} \mid q \mid (2N - p) \}.
\]
Because every complement is composite and odd, its prime factors are strictly bounded by $q \le \frac{2N-p}{3} \le \frac{2N-5}{3}$. The sequence of sets $P^*(n+1) = D(P^*(n))$ forms a non-increasing chain of finite sets that stabilizes in finitely many steps at a non-empty stationary limit set $P^*_\infty = D(P^*_\infty)$.

### 3. Minimal Terminal Islands as Irreducible Digraphs
Inside $P^*_\infty$, every minimal terminal component $I$ of cardinality $k = |I|$ forms an irreducible, strongly connected directed graph $G = (I, R)$ governed by an exponent matrix $M \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}^{k \times k}$ with zero diagonal ($\operatorname{Tr}(M) = 0$) and spectral radius $\rho(M) \ge 2$:
\[
2N - p_i = \prod_{j=1}^k p_j^{a_{i,j}}, \quad \forall i \in \{1, \dots, k\}.
\]

### 4. Low-Dimensional Collapse ($k \le 5$)
- **$k = 0$:** Contradicts the finite convergence to a non-empty stationary limit set (Proposition 2.14).
- **$k = 1$:** Forces $p \mid (2N - p) \implies p \mid 2N$, impossible since $P^*(0)$ excludes factors of $2N$ (Proposition 4.1).
- **$k = 2$:** Two coprime prime powers $2N - p_1 = p_2^{a_1}$ and $2N - p_2 = p_1^{a_2}$ violate Baker's linear forms in logarithms (Proposition 4.2).
- **$k = 3$:** Strong connectivity forces a 3-cycle or Hamiltonian configuration whose modular and logarithmic gaps are incompatible with integer exponents (Proposition 4.3).
- **$k = 4, 5$:** Maximal prime in-degree rigidity ($\operatorname{in-deg}(p_k) = 1, a_{m,k} = 1$) triggers a descending prime power cascade that over-constrains the lower primes, violating Baker--Matveev linear form bounds (Propositions 4.14, 4.15).
- **Square-Free Domain ($a_{i,j} \in \{0, 1\}$):** Unconditionally eliminated for all $k \ge 4$ via spectral gap contraction (Proposition 4.5).

### 5. Inductive Node Contraction & Asymptotic Ceiling ($k \ge 98$)
- By maximal in-degree rigidity, the largest prime $p_k$ divides exactly one complement $2N - p_m$ with exponent $1$: $p_k = \frac{2N - p_m}{Q_m}$.
- Substituting $p_k$ into the factorization of $2N - p_k$ eliminates node $k$ while **strictly preserving the total exponent mass** $\sum_{i,j} A_{i,j} \ge 2k$.
- Successive contraction down to dimension $5$ produces a $5 \times 5$ matrix whose mean row sum satisfies $\overline{S}_5 \ge \frac{2k}{5}$.
- The pigeonhole principle forces at least one row product $\ge 3^{2k/5}$. For $k > \frac{5}{2} \lfloor \frac{\ln(2N)}{\ln 3} \rfloor$, this compressed product exceeds $2N$, creating an absolute magnitude contradiction.
- For $2N \approx 4 \cdot 10^{18}$, this unconditionally eliminates all fixed-point islands of dimension $k \ge 98$ (Proposition 4.17).

### 6. The Surviving Frontier ($6 \le k \le 97$)
- For intermediate dimensions $6 \le k \le 97$:
  - If $p_2 > \sqrt{2N}$, it is unconditionally eliminated by elementary complement growth (Proposition 4.16).
  - If $p_2 \le \sqrt{2N}$, the two coprime factorizations must satisfy $|\Lambda_{1,2}| < 10^{-9}$ (Proposition 4.16).
- The complete resolution of Goldbach's Conjecture is thus reduced to the **Spectral Decoupling Conjecture (Conjecture 5.1)** across the finite window $6 \le k \le 97$.
